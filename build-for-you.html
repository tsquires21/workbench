No. 11 · For you

Got a problem like these?

Everything in this notebook started the same way: a real, specific annoyance that no off-the-shelf app quite fixed. If you've got one of those in your business, here are the kinds of things I'd enjoy exploring with you. Each one is traced back to something already built and working in these pages.

Crews & jobs

A system your crew will actually use

Jobs at addresses, each person's day on their phone, check-lists and photos as proof, and an office view of what's late and what's billable. It's shaped around how your crew really works.

Already built: the business system, which runs separate businesses side by side.
Public data

Free public data, turned into a clear answer

Weather, satellite, county property records and laser scans, narrowed to the exact places that matter to you: how much sun a spot gets, whether a storm crossed an address, or which properties fit a profile.

Already built: the sun map and the lightning watcher.
In the field

A calculator that works with no signal

Your trade's math (quantities, materials, waste) in a phone app that works in a basement or on a rural site, with every formula written down and checked against numbers you trust.

Already built: the trade calculators, six apps on one tested engine.
Reporting

Messy data, cleaned up and answerable

Spreadsheets and exports from a few systems, reconciled into one place, so the questions that take an afternoon today take a minute. This is my day job's home turf.

Background: my day job as a senior data analyst and integration specialist (SQL, Power BI, Python).
How I'd go about it
Talk firstAn hour on how your work actually goes, and where the time and money leak out.
Small and realA working prototype of one piece, using your real data, before anyone spends real money.
Use it on real jobsYou and your crew use it for a couple of weeks. What breaks tells us what to build next.
You own itFree and open-source parts wherever possible, so you're not renting your own data back.

Who you'd be working with. I'm one person with a day job I like. I build in the evenings and on weekends, designing, deciding and testing while an AI partner does a lot of the typing. That makes me quick at small, specific tools that fit exactly how you work. For a big system that needs a team on call around the clock, I'd tell you so up front.

Want to talk?

Bring the problem that eats your week. That's usually the right place to start.
