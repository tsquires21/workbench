No. 09 · How I build

How one person ships all this.

I have a day job I enjoy, and none of this existed in April. Everything in this notebook happened on evenings and weekends, in under five months. The trick isn't working more hours. It's a personal system, which I call my AI operating system, that does the legwork around the clock and brings me decisions instead of chores.

The rule that makes it trustworthy: the AI proposes, and I decide. Nothing it does overnight goes live until I've read it.

The pieces
ResearchRoland, the research agent"Roland, go learn about X." He asks clarifying questions first, searches widely, and returns findings with sources, facts only, no opinions. Plenty of what's in this notebook started as one of his reports.
OvernightThe research queueDrop a topic in a list from my phone during the day. Two runs overnight research it and leave a written brief with citations for the morning.
OvernightNight shiftsA builder works through a ranked to-do list while I sleep. It only proposes: each change waits as a reviewable patch, and I approve or reject it with a tap.
6:45 amThe morning briefWhat came in overnight, what's stale, and the single best first move of the day, waiting for me with my coffee.
ScoutsReuse before buildingRadars scan open-source projects and a few trusted YouTube channels, and brief only the handful that matter. The first question for any new idea is "does this already exist?"
HostingA server in the houseI run the infrastructure too. A small, always-on Linux box at home serves the live pieces, like the lightning watcher and the D&D ledger, to anyone's phone. This site is published free on GitHub Pages. There are no monthly bills.
GatekeeperThe roastBefore I invest in an idea, five AI critics attack it from different angles (contrarian, first-principles, future maintainer, and more), and a judge returns green, reshape, or kill.
MeDecide what's worth buildingIdeas get captured, then roasted, then scoped

What "built with AI" actually means here

I read code, but I don't hand-type most of it anymore. My job is the part AI is bad at: knowing what's worth building, noticing when an answer is confidently wrong, and testing against reality.

The pages in this notebook are full of those moments: the heat rule that was really a different rule, the laser scan that remembered a tree that's gone, the money bugs a green test suite missed. Catching those is the job.

Under the hood
  • Claude Code as the build partner, driven by a git repository of instructions, skills and project notes, so the system remembers the work across days and devices.
  • Scheduled tasks on a Windows laptop run the headless shifts. An always-on Linux mini-server at home ("hearth") serves the public pages and the lightning watcher.
  • Built to survive without the AI. Every tool is rated on whether it would still run if the AI went away. Most of what's in this notebook would, because the AI helped build them but isn't needed to run them.
  • Free and open-source wherever possible, local-first, and running on hardware I own.
  • About 40 projects and roughly 4,800 automated tests across 16 test suites.
